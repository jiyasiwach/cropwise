# Cotton leaf curl virus disease

## Signs on the leaf

Small-vein thickening on young upper leaves first, then upward or downward leaf curling, cup-shaped leafy outgrowths (enations) on the leaf underside; plants stunted with less flowering and fruiting. Usually appears 45-55 days after sowing (end of June), mostly in North India.

## What favours it

Spread by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci); weed and crop alternate hosts carry the virus; susceptible G. hirsutum varieties in endemic areas; excess nitrogen; late sowing (May first-fortnight sowing escapes it).

## What to do

- Avoid susceptible varieties in endemic areas; sow resistant varieties/hybrids (page lists H-1117, H-1226, H-1236, F-1861, LH-2076, RS-875, RS-810, RS-2013, LHH-144, CSHH-198, CSHH-238, CSHH-243).
- Sow desi cotton (G. arboreum) in hot-spot areas.
- Sow in the first fortnight of May.
- Remove weed hosts in season and off season (Sida, Abutilon, Ageratum, Parthenium, Solanum nigrum, Lantana, etc.).
- Do not grow okra in or around cotton; do not grow American cotton near citrus orchards.
- Destroy volunteer/ratoon cotton off-season; destroy/uproot affected plants (North India).
- Avoid excess nitrogen.
- Yellow sticky traps for mass-trapping whitefly.
- Quarantine: restrict movement of diseased plant material.

## Sprays named by the source

The source names these treatments, quoted exactly. Use them only after your KVK confirms the diagnosis and the dose for your crop stage:

- "The following insecticides may alternatively be used: neem formulations i.e Azadirachtin 0.03% (300 ppm) (Neem Oil Based WSP) or Azadirachtin 0.15% W/W (Neem Seed Kernel Based EC) @ 2.5-5.0 l/ha, or Triazophos 40% EC @1.5 l/ha or Ethion 50% EC@ 2.0 l/ha"
- "Avoid use of synthetic pyrethroids when whitefly population exists"
